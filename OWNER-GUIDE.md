# Hearth TV — how to use it

One app for the TV, the Sonos and the Roku, on your phone, on your own Wi-Fi. Nothing runs in the background,
nothing leaves the house, and there is no account to make.

## Install it

1. On your phone, open **https://willits-alec.github.io/hearth-tv/**
2. Tap **Download Hearth TV**.
3. Open the download. If Chrome asks whether it may install unknown apps, allow it this once.
4. If a warning about an unknown developer appears, choose **Install anyway**.
5. Open **Hearth TV**. Make sure the phone is on the home Wi-Fi.

## The first minute

The app walks through five steps and remembers everything afterwards.

1. **Find the TV.** It looks by itself. If nothing appears, turn the TV on and tap *Search again*.
2. **Pair with the TV.** The TV puts a four-digit code on screen. Type it in and tap *Pair*. This happens once,
   ever. If no code appears, there is a *pre-shared key* option lower down, but you should not need it.
3. **Find the Sonos.** Pick the sound bar. The volume buttons will then drive the Sonos directly.
4. **Find the Roku.** Pick it if you have one, or tap *Skip*.
5. **All set.** Tap *Open the remote*.

## The remote

**The top line** says what the TV is doing: *On*, what is playing, or *Standby*. The round button beside it turns
the TV on and off. When the TV is off, that button is all you need.

**Volume** drives the Sonos whenever the TV's sound is set to the sound bar, which is the normal state. The line
above the buttons always says which one it is driving. Hold a volume button to keep going.

> The TV will **not** show its own volume bar on screen while the app is driving the Sonos. That is expected. The
> app talks straight to the sound bar, so the TV never hears about it. The number in the app is the sound bar's
> own answer, so if the number moves, the volume moved.

**Inputs** lists what is plugged in by name, so the Roku and the sound bar appear as themselves rather than as
HDMI numbers. Empty sockets are hidden.

**Apps** shows the ones you actually use. Tap *All* for the full list.

**Navigate** is the arrow pad, with OK in the middle and Back, Home, Menu and Info beneath. Hold an arrow to
repeat it. **More keys** opens the rest of the remote: the guide, channel up and down, the number keys, subtitles
and the coloured buttons.

**Sound** has one button worth knowing: **Fix the sound**. If the TV is playing through its own speakers, or the
sound bar is sitting on the wrong input, that one tap puts both back the way they should be. The night-sound and
speech-enhancement switches are the sound bar's own settings.

**Type on the TV** sends what you type on the phone to a text box on the TV, which is far quicker than the arrow
keys for a search or a password. Open the box on the TV first, then send. The app tells you if no box is open.

**The microphone** at the top takes a spoken instruction: *volume up*, *open Prime*, *switch to Roku*, *pause*,
*fix the sound*. It repeats back what it understood.

**Roku** appears as its own section once you have picked the box. Tapping *Switch to Roku* or a channel also
switches the TV over for you, so you never have to change the input first.

## Updates

When there is a newer version, a banner appears at the top of the remote. Tap **Download**, then install it over
the top. Your pairing and settings survive. You can also just revisit the page.

## If something looks wrong

Tap the **spanner** at the top for Diagnostics.

- **Run self-test** checks the TV, the sound bar, the Roku and the connection, and marks each one pass or fail. A
  failure says what to do about it.
- **Recent errors** lists the last twenty things that went wrong, with times.
- **Copy report** puts all of that on the clipboard. Paste it into a message to Alec and he can see what happened
  without being in the room.
- **Add the Roku** and **Add the Sonos** let you attach a device later without pairing the TV again.

Two things that are normal and not faults:

- Nothing works when the phone is off the home Wi-Fi. The app says so. It is a local remote by design.
- If the TV's address changes, the app finds it again on its own within a minute.

## The short version

| I want to | Do this |
| --- | --- |
| Turn the TV on | The round button, top right |
| Change the volume | The volume buttons; hold to keep going |
| No sound, or sound from the wrong speakers | **Fix the sound** |
| Watch something | Tap it in **Apps**, or a channel under **Roku** |
| Search without the arrow keys | Open the search box on the TV, then **Type on the TV** |
| Tell it what to do out loud | The microphone, top of the screen |
| Something is broken | Spanner, **Run self-test**, **Copy report**, send it to Alec |
